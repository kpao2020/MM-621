# Chapter 4 Self Reflection — **Criticism, Function, and Discipline**

**Key point:** Critical design should be discussed as design, with attention to how objects work through their use, social context, and debate — not only through practical function or art criticism.

#### Criticism and the Social Context of Design

In Chapter 4, Malpass explains that critical design is often evaluated through ideas from art and visual culture. This can make people focus on whether a project looks like art instead of asking what it is doing as design. He argues that critical design should be considered in relation to the everyday objects, habits, and systems that it questions. Malpass writes that 
> “objects need to be viewed as industrial design” (2017, p. 76). 

To me, this means that a design should be judged in the context it is trying to affect, not only by how it appears in a gallery.

The examples in the chapter also show that design can bring people together around a social issue. The Energy Babble project, for example, uses a familiar household object to start conversations about energy use. I found this interesting because the object does not need to solve the whole problem by itself. It can help people notice the issue, share their views, and consider what might change. This makes me think of design as a way to ask questions and create discussion, as well as a way to make products for daily use.

#### Function Beyond Practical Use

A main argument in this chapter is that an object's function does not only mean that an object performs a practical task efficiently. but should also carries out different kinds of meanings, including physical, psychological, social, and cultural meanings. Malpass explains that an object’s function can change depending on who uses it, where it is used, and what meaning people give it. As he writes, 
> “Function is a dynamic quality and open to wilful appropriation” (2017, p. 89-90).

This idea made me reconsider how I judge whether a design works. A product may not have an obvious practical use, but it can still work by communicating an idea or making people think about their choices. The user also affects the meaning of the object through how they understand and use it. I agree with this broader view, though I think designers still have to give people enough context to understand what the object is inviting them to consider. If the message is unclear, people might miss the point or misunderstand the meaning.

#### Critical Design as Inquiry

Malpass connects critical design to research and inquiry. Instead of beginning with a specific user need and trying to solve it, a designer might create an object or scenario that asks “what if?” This can help people see assumptions that are usually taken for granted. The chapter also describes critical design as working near the boundaries of the design discipline. It can question familiar design goals, such as efficiency and market success, while still using the methods and knowledge of product design.

I think this is valuable because design problems are not always simple or ready for a direct solution. Sometimes people need to understand the problem differently before they can decide what to do. A design that starts a conversation can be useful for that reason. At the same time, Malpass points out that critical design should still be open to criticism. If a project is meant to create debate, that does not automatically mean the project communicates clearly or has a strong effect.

#### A New Perspective

This chapter helped me see that function and design can have more than one meaning. An object can perform a practical task, but it can also shape relationships, represent values, and encourage people to question a situation. Critical design can be part of product design when it uses objects and scenarios to explore concerns that reach beyond the object itself.

Moving forward, I will think about what a design does in the real context where people encounter it, what meanings they may attach to it, and whether it gives them a reason to reflect or respond. I will also remember that a design can ask a useful question, but it still needs to communicate that question in a way people can engage with.
